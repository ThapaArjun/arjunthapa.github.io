I study the ecology, distribution and conservation of threatened wildlife in the Himalayas. My research combines field surveys, spatial modelling and conservation genetics to understand how environmental change affects species and their habitats.

About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Wildlife Conservation at the Institute of Forestry, Hetauda Campus, Tribhuvan University, Nepal. My work focuses on understanding and conserving threatened mammals, with a particular interest in red pandas and Himalayan biodiversity.

I completed my PhD in Ecology at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, conducting research at the Institute of Zoology on red panda conservation ecology and genetics across the species’ geographic range. I subsequently undertook postdoctoral research at the Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, through the President’s International Fellowship Initiative.

My research connects ecological fieldwork with species distribution modelling, spatial analysis and genetic approaches. Alongside research, I teach wildlife conservation, supervise students and contribute to conservation planning and community-based wildlife monitoring.

Research

Red Panda Ecology and Conservation

I investigate red panda distribution, habitat requirements, diet and conservation threats across the Himalayas. My work also examines genetic diversity and evolutionary history to inform conservation priorities for red panda populations.

Climate Change and Biodiversity

I study how climate and land-cover changes influence threatened species and their habitats. Through species distribution modelling, I explore potential range shifts, climate refugia and areas that may be important for long-term conservation.

Habitat Connectivity and Wildlife Corridors

My research examines habitat occupancy, land-cover change and the conservation value of wildlife corridors. This includes work in Nepal’s Laljhadi–Mohana biological corridor and other landscapes connecting wildlife habitats.

Human–Wildlife Coexistence

I investigate patterns of human–wildlife conflict, including leopard attacks, livestock depredation and interactions with primates. My aim is to support practical mitigation measures that account for both wildlife conservation and local livelihoods.

Camera Trapping and Biodiversity Data

I contribute to collaborative efforts to mobilize and share camera-trap data from Asia. These initiatives improve access to biodiversity information for ecological research, monitoring and conservation planning.

Selected Conservation Projects

Research and consultancy examining climate change impacts on Nepal’s fauna, species distributions and habitats.
