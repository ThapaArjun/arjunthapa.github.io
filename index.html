Dr Arjun Thapa

Assistant Professor of Wildlife Conservation

Institute of Forestry, Hetauda Campus · Tribhuvan University, Nepal

I study the ecology and conservation of threatened wildlife in the Himalayas. My research combines field surveys, spatial modelling and conservation genetics to understand how environmental change affects species and their habitats.

Email Research Publications

About Me

My academic work connects ecological research with conservation practice, student mentorship and community-based wildlife monitoring in Nepal.

I completed my PhD in Ecology at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, conducting research at the Institute of Zoology. I subsequently undertook postdoctoral research at the Chinese Academy of Sciences through the President’s International Fellowship Initiative.

Alongside research, I teach wildlife conservation, supervise students and contribute to conservation planning and community-based wildlife monitoring.

Research

Red Panda Conservation

Understanding distribution, habitat requirements, genetic diversity and conservation threats across the Himalayan region.

Climate Change and Biodiversity

Assessing species vulnerability, potential range shifts and climate refugia under environmental change.

Wildlife Corridors

Studying habitat occupancy, landscape connectivity and land-cover change to inform corridor conservation.

Human–Wildlife Coexistence

Investigating wildlife conflict and practical mitigation approaches that support conservation and local livelihoods.
